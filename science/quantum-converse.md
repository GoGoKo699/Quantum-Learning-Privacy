# How much information can one private quantum report retain?

The collective identification protocol uses order $`d\log d`$ specimens at fixed privacy. The converse here shows that this order is optimal among **noninteractive product encoders**, even when the quantum decoder is unrestricted. It does not restrict the dimension of a report. It does not establish the optimum for encoders chosen adaptively from previous reports, or a matching lower bound for clean-test prediction alone.

## The experiment and the statement

Write $`d=2^n`$, $`M=d^2-1`$, and let $`\mathcal P_*`$ be the phase-fixed nonidentity Hermitian $`n`$-qubit Paulis. The hidden target $`H`$ is uniform on the $`L=2M`$ signed alternatives $`\{\pm P:P\in\mathcal P_*\}`$. A specimen with public fair label $`y\in\{\pm1\}`$ is


```math
\rho_{H,y}=\frac{I+yH}{d}.
```


The labels and all public random coins are independent of $`H`$. They may determine the entire encoder sequence. Conditional on this public information, the $`j`$th owner applies a predetermined one-specimen CPTP channel $`\Lambda_j`$. For every pair of input density matrices, each such conditional channel satisfies


```math
\Lambda_j(\rho)\preceq e^\epsilon\Lambda_j(\sigma).
\tag{Q1}
```


The encoder is a product of these local channels: private random choices and local environments are fresh for each specimen. Shared public randomness is allowed. A hidden key or quantum memory shared between local encoders is outside this product model. A public flag generated by a local channel must be included in that channel's output when checking (Q1); checking privacy only after discarding the flag would be a different promise.

After receiving all $`N`$ reports, the decoder may use any joint measurement and unlimited computation to produce $`\widehat H`$.

**Theorem Q: noninteractive quantum identification converse.** For $`\epsilon>0`$ and $`0\le\delta\le1-1/L`$, if $`\Pr[\widehat H\ne H]\le\delta`$, then


```math
N\ge
\frac{d-1}{(e^\epsilon-1)^2}
\left[(1-\delta)\log\bigl(2(d^2-1)\bigr)-h_2(\delta)\right],
\tag{Q2}
```


where logarithms are natural and $`h_2(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)`$. In particular, at any fixed positive privacy level and fixed $`\delta<1`$, bounded-error identification needs $`\Omega_\epsilon(d\log d)`$ specimens as $`d\to\infty`$. A worst-case error promise implies the average-error promise used here.

The denominator in (Q2) may be replaced by the smaller $`4\sinh^2(\epsilon/2)`$. We prove the displayed, simpler form first. At $`\epsilon=0`$ every report is independent of its input, so identification with error below $`1-1/L`$ is impossible for every finite $`N`$.

## One report: support and a quadratic information budget

Fix one conditional encoder $`\Lambda`$, and put


```math
\tau=\Lambda(I/d),\qquad A_P=\Lambda(P)/d.
```


Averaging (Q1) over the comparison input gives, for every input state $`\rho`$,


```math
e^{-\epsilon}\tau\preceq\Lambda(\rho)\preceq e^\epsilon\tau.
\tag{Q3}
```


Therefore every report, and every $`A_P`$, is supported on $`\operatorname{supp}\tau`$. All inverse powers below are taken only on this support; zero eigenvalues outside it cause no inversion. For any pure state $`\psi=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|`$, define


```math
R_\psi=\tau^{-1/2}\Lambda(\psi)\tau^{-1/2}.
```


Equation (Q3) gives $`e^{-\epsilon}I\preceq R_\psi\preceq e^\epsilon I`$. Consequently


```math
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\tau(R_\psi-I)^2\right]
\le (e^\epsilon-1)^2.
\tag{Q4}
```


This is an operator bound followed by a trace against $`\tau`$; it does not assume that $`\tau`$ and $`R_\psi`$ commute.

The normalized Pauli expansion of a pure state is


```math
\psi=\frac1d\left(I+\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_*}a_P(\psi)P\right),
\qquad a_P(\psi)=\operatorname{Tr}(P\psi).
```


For Haar-uniform pure $`\psi`$, the standard second-moment identity gives


```math
\mathbb E_\psi[a_P(\psi)a_Q(\psi)]
=\frac{\delta_{P,Q}}{d+1}.
\tag{Q5}
```


Indeed, $`\mathbb E\psi^{\otimes2}=(I+F)/[d(d+1)]`$, while $`\operatorname{Tr}P=0`$ and $`\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=d\delta_{P,Q}`$. A pure-state projective 2-design would give the same equality.

Set $`B_\psi=\Lambda(\psi)-\tau=\sum_P a_P(\psi)A_P`$. Cyclicity of the trace gives the exact identity


```math
\operatorname{Tr}(B_\psi\tau^{-1}B_\psi)
=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\tau(R_\psi-I)^2\right].
\tag{Q6}
```


Taking the Haar average, using (Q5), and then (Q4) proves


```math
\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_*}
\operatorname{Tr}(A_P\tau^{-1}A_P)
\le(d+1)(e^\epsilon-1)^2.
\tag{Q7}
```


Every summand is nonnegative: it equals the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of $`\tau^{-1/2}A_P`$. Equation (Q7) is the useful dimension-dependent budget. Privacy bounds the response to every pure input, and the second moment distributes that response among the $`d^2-1`$ Pauli directions.

For the stated improvement of the constant, let $`l=e^{-\epsilon}`$ and $`u=e^\epsilon`$. Since $`(R_\psi-lI)(uI-R_\psi)\succeq0`$ and $`\operatorname{Tr}(\tau R_\psi)=1`$, one has


```math
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\tau(R_\psi-I)^2\right]
\le (u-1)(1-l)=4\sinh^2(\epsilon/2).
```


The rest of the proof is unchanged.

## From the quadratic budget to relative entropy

For density matrices $`\omega,\tau`$ with $`\operatorname{supp}\omega\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\tau`$, the standard order-two Petz bound is


```math
D(\omega\Vert\tau)
\le \log\operatorname{Tr}(\omega^2\tau^{-1})
\le \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\omega-\tau)\tau^{-1}(\omega-\tau)\right].
\tag{Q8}
```


Here is a direct verification that keeps noncommutation explicit. Diagonalize $`\omega=\sum_i p_i|u_i\rangle\langle u_i|`$ and $`\tau=\sum_j q_j|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|`$ on the relevant supports. The weights $`p_i|\langle u_i|v_j\rangle|^2`$ sum to one, and


```math
D(\omega\Vert\tau)
=\sum_{i,j}p_i|\langle u_i|v_j\rangle|^2\log\frac{p_i}{q_j}.
```


Concavity of $`\log`$ bounds this by


```math
\log\sum_{i,j}\frac{p_i^2}{q_j}|\langle u_i|v_j\rangle|^2
=\log\operatorname{Tr}(\omega^2\tau^{-1}).
```


Finally, $`\log x\le x-1`$ and $`\operatorname{Tr}\omega=\operatorname{Tr}\tau=1`$ give the second inequality in (Q8). The quadratic expression is the Petz chi-squared divergence; it is not a commuting-state substitution.

For a fixed public label $`y`$, the output for $`H=sP`$ is $`\omega_{sP}=\tau+ysA_P`$. Its average over uniform signed $`H`$ is exactly $`\tau`$. Applying (Q8) to every alternative and then (Q7),


```math
\begin{aligned}
\chi(H:Q)
&=\frac1{2M}\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_*}\sum_{s=\pm1}
D(\tau+ysA_P\Vert\tau)\\
&\le\frac1M\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_*}
\operatorname{Tr}(A_P\tau^{-1}A_P)\\
&\le\frac{(e^\epsilon-1)^2}{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{Q9}
```


The factor $`1/(d-1)`$ follows from $`(d+1)/(d^2-1)=1/(d-1)`$. Output dimension has not entered the bound.

## Many reports: use a product reference, not a product ensemble mean

Condition on the entire public label string and public random coins. The target remains uniform because these variables are independent of $`H`$. Let $`\omega_{h,j}`$ be the $`j`$th conditional report and $`\tau_j=\Lambda_j(I/d)`$. The product-encoder promise gives


```math
\omega_h^{(N)}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^N\omega_{h,j},
\qquad T=\bigotimes_{j=1}^N\tau_j.
```


The ensemble mean $`\bar\omega^{(N)}=L^{-1}\sum_h\omega_h^{(N)}`$ is generally correlated. We do not replace it by $`T`$. Instead, the relative-entropy decomposition gives exactly


```math
\frac1L\sum_hD(\omega_h^{(N)}\Vert T)
=\chi(H:Q^N)+D(\bar\omega^{(N)}\Vert T)
\ge\chi(H:Q^N).
\tag{Q10}
```


Relative entropy is additive on tensor products. Equations (Q9)–(Q10) therefore imply


```math
\chi(H:Q^N\mid\text{public information})
\le\frac{N(e^\epsilon-1)^2}{d-1}.
\tag{Q11}
```


The same bound holds after averaging over all public information, including correlated public coins and label-dependent encoder choices. Public flags genuinely included in each private output are covered by the one-channel argument itself. The proof does not re-use a uniform posterior after an adaptive measurement history.

## Identification and the sample lower bound

Holevo's bound and data processing bound the mutual information in any final decoded estimate by (Q11). Fano's inequality for the uniform $`L`$-valued target gives


```math
I(H;\widehat H)\ge\log L-h_2(\delta)-\delta\log(L-1)
\ge(1-\delta)\log L-h_2(\delta).
```


Combining the two inequalities proves (Q2). The argument permits different predetermined encoders for different specimens and arbitrary joint decoding; identical encoding is not required.

All displayed matrix arguments hold for any finite report dimension, with no dimension-dependent constant. The same conclusion holds for separable infinite-dimensional report spaces: for any finite $`N`$ and finite target ensemble, compress each local output to an increasing finite-dimensional subspace plus an erasure flag. This is CPTP, preserves (Q1), and approximates every target output in trace norm. The original decoder can then be approximated on these compressed outputs. Apply the dimension-independent finite-dimensional inequality and take the compression error to zero.

## What this establishes

At fixed positive $`\epsilon`$, the identification protocol's $`O_\epsilon(d\log d)`$ specimen order matches this lower bound in the noninteractive product-encoder model. Quantum outputs and collective decoding are fully allowed in the converse. No claim of a matching clean-prediction lower bound follows: a classifier can have nonzero predictive advantage while identifying only a bounded list of candidate Paulis. Nor does this proof cover quantum encoders selected using previous private outcomes, coherent feedback to an owner, collective processing of raw specimens, or reused private encoder keys.

The tools in this proof are inherited: the Haar/projective-2-design second moment, the Petz order-two relative-entropy bound, the relative-entropy decomposition of Holevo information, Holevo's measurement bound, and classical Fano's inequality. The task-specific step is combining their exact normalizations with all-input local privacy to obtain (Q7) and the per-report $`1/(d-1)`$ information budget. Standard relative-entropy and Holevo foundations are presented in John Watrous, [*The Theory of Quantum Information*](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/TQI/), Chapter 5. The proof above supplies the needed calculation rather than treating these ingredients as new results.
